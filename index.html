Cartoon

Playful, exaggerated characters and comic-style linework, usually in bold color or clean black outlines. A favorite for pop-culture tributes and lighthearted pieces. Suits anyone who wants their tattoo to spark a smile rather than make a serious statement.

3D

Shading and perspective tricks that make the design look like it's popping off, or cut into, the skin. Technically demanding and slower to complete than most styles. Draws in clients who want a striking, technically impressive statement piece.

Blackwork

Solid black ink and heavy saturation, built from bold pattern work or graphic silhouettes. One of the most durable styles, since it tends to hold up better over time than color work. Favored by clients who want high-contrast pieces that read clearly from across a room.

Continuous Line

A single unbroken line forms the entire design, minimal and deliberate. Quick to tattoo, but unforgiving of any wobble, so it demands a steady hand. Appeals to minimalists who want something quiet, refined, and easy to place almost anywhere.

Geometric

Precise shapes, symmetry, and repeating patterns — mandalas, sacred geometry, or pure abstract linework. Success lives or dies on careful measuring and steady linework. Popular with clients drawn to structure, balance, and clean, mathematical design.

Fine Line

Delicate, thin needlework producing soft, understated designs. It tends to soften with age faster than bold work, so touch-ups come sooner. A common first-tattoo choice for clients who want something quiet and elegant rather than loud.

Loose, hand-drawn linework that mimics a pencil sketch, cross-hatching and rough edges included on purpose. It's meant to look like a living drawing rather than a polished illustration. Attracts clients who like that intentionally unfinished, artistic feel.

Micro

Tribal

Bold black patterns built from thick curving lines and negative space, rooted in Polynesian, Maori, and other Indigenous tattooing traditions. One of the oldest continuously practiced styles in the world. Clients drawn to this work should talk directly with the artist about the pattern's meaning and origin before booking.

Realism

Photographic detail, shading, and depth meant to look like a real photo on skin — portraits, animals, objects. It takes the most time and skill of any style here, often across multiple sessions. Sought after by clients wanting a tribute piece or a hyper-detailed statement.

Our Story

Why Bone & Ember exists

Bone & Ember started because a number of working artists in wildly different styles kept hearing the same complaint: most studios make you pick one "house style" and stick with it. We built something different — a single studio where traditional, fine line, realism, and everything in between share the same walls, so clients pick the artist who fits the work, not the other way around.

Based in Texas, working by appointment, and built around a booking process that actually respects your time — no walking in blind, no guessing which artist to ask for.
